# Explain Like I'm Five

> If you can't explain it to a child, you don't truly understand it.
>
> — Naval Ravikant

During a workshop at a client's offices, we explored the concepts behind video streaming and how to explain them in the simplest possible terms. We came up with examples that could be used to explain each concept to a five-year-old.

## Client-side vs server-side adverts

- Think of a colouring book versus a blank drawing book.
- A colouring book already has the pictures printed in it. That makes colouring in easier, but limits what you can create.
- A blank drawing book lets you draw whatever you like, with no guide lines.

## Feature flags (LaunchDarkly)

- Mr. Potato Head lets you change what he looks like. Want to add ears for a bit? Add ears. Want to try a hat to see if it looks good? Add a hat.
- LaunchDarkly is a service that lets you switch features on and off in the same way.

## Video players (Shaka / THEOplayer / XDK)

- Imagine you're drawing a picture on an iPad. XDK gives you a fixed set of brushes and colours, which limits your options.
- Shaka and THEOplayer can be set up to give you a much wider choice of brushes, colours, and effects.

## Transcoding

- Imagine taking a big comic book and cutting it into small strips to print in newspapers.
- You can change content from high resolution to low resolution, change its format, or change its language, depending on what the audience needs.
- You can even fold something up so it fits into a smaller space.

## Analytics

- A friend watches you while you do a task, keeping an eye on how you're getting on so they can help if something goes wrong.

## Variants

- People with different-sized feet need different-sized shoes.
- If you give an adult a child's shoe, they won't be able to wear it, so we tailor things to each client's needs.
- You also wear different shoes depending on what you're doing: trainers for going outside (for example, mobile) and slippers for indoors (for example, connected TV).

## Digital Rights Management (DRM)

- Imagine you're writing in your diary. Depending on what you've written, you might protect it so nobody else can read it:
  - A diary with no lock
  - A diary with a padlock and key
  - A diary with a padlock and key, locked inside a safe
- Coded messages can only be read by the person they're meant for.

## Content Delivery Network (CDN)

- Picture an ice cream factory that needs to get ice cream to the vans that sell it. Big lorries can carry more at once (bandwidth).
- Ice cream is stored in local depots close to the customers (caching).
- Ice cream vans drive around and sell to customers.
- If the ice cream melts, it took too long to arrive (latency).
